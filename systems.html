Human Body Systems

Structured educational overviews of the 11 major organ systems. Each entry includes overview, anatomy, main organs, functions, and scientific context.

Skeletal System

Overview: Provides structural support, protection, and levers for movement; stores minerals and produces blood cells.

Anatomy: Adult skeleton contains 206 bones. Axial (skull, vertebral column, thoracic cage) and appendicular (limbs and girdles).

Main organs/structures: Skull, vertebrae, ribs, sternum, femur, pelvis.

Physiological functions: Support, protection of vital organs, movement (with muscles), mineral homeostasis (Ca²⁺, PO₄³⁻), hematopoiesis in red marrow.

Cellular & biochemical notes: Osteoblasts, osteocytes, osteoclasts. Matrix: ~30% organic (type I collagen) + ~70% inorganic hydroxyapatite.

Scientific facts: Bone is dynamic; remodeling occurs throughout life. Peak bone mass is reached in early adulthood.

References: OpenStax Anatomy & Physiology; NCBI Bookshelf — Bone Histology.

Muscular System

Overview: Enables movement, maintains posture, and generates heat.

Anatomy: Three types — skeletal (voluntary, striated), cardiac (heart), smooth (visceral, involuntary).

Main structures: Skeletal muscles (e.g., biceps brachii, quadriceps), diaphragm, myocardium.

Functions: Locomotion, joint stabilization, heat production (thermogenesis), blood circulation (cardiac), organ motility (smooth).

Cellular notes: Sarcomeres in striated muscle; actin-myosin sliding filament mechanism. ATP required for cross-bridge cycling.

References: OpenStax Anatomy & Physiology; standard muscle physiology texts.

Nervous System

Overview: Coordinates rapid responses, processes sensory information, and enables cognition.

Anatomy: Central (brain + spinal cord) and peripheral (cranial/spinal nerves, ganglia).

Main organs: Cerebrum, cerebellum, brainstem, spinal cord.

Functions: Sensory integration, motor control, homeostasis, higher functions (learning, memory).

Cellular notes: Neurons transmit action potentials; synapses use neurotransmitters (e.g., acetylcholine, glutamate, GABA).

References: OpenStax Anatomy & Physiology; NCBI Neuroscience resources.

Cardiovascular System

Overview: Transports blood, gases, nutrients, hormones, and waste products.

Anatomy: Heart (4 chambers), arteries, veins, capillaries. Pulmonary and systemic circuits.

Main organ: Heart. Sinoatrial node generates rhythm.

Functions: Delivery of oxygen and nutrients; removal of CO₂ and metabolic waste; thermoregulation support.

Biochemical notes: Hemoglobin binds O₂; carbonic anhydrase facilitates CO₂ transport. Blood pressure regulated by neural and hormonal mechanisms.

References: OpenStax; Guyton & Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology (selected concepts).

Respiratory System

Overview: Facilitates gas exchange between air and blood.

Anatomy: Upper (nasal cavity, pharynx, larynx) and lower (trachea, bronchi, lungs with alveoli).

Main organs: Lungs. Alveolar surface area ~70 m².

Functions: Oxygen uptake, carbon dioxide elimination, pH regulation support, vocalization.

Cellular notes: Type I and Type II pneumocytes; surfactant reduces surface tension.

References: OpenStax Anatomy & Physiology.

Digestive System

Overview: Ingests, digests, absorbs nutrients, and eliminates waste.

Anatomy: Alimentary canal (mouth to anus) + accessory organs (liver, pancreas, gallbladder).

Main organs: Stomach, small intestine, large intestine, liver, pancreas.

Functions: Mechanical and chemical digestion, absorption of nutrients and water, microbiome support.

Biochemical notes: Enzymes (amylase, pepsin, lipase, proteases); bile emulsifies fats; pancreatic bicarbonate neutralizes acid.

References: OpenStax; NCBI digestive physiology.

Endocrine System

Overview: Regulates long-term processes via hormones.

Anatomy: Glands including pituitary, thyroid, parathyroid, adrenal, pancreas (islets), gonads.

Functions: Growth, metabolism, reproduction, stress response, calcium homeostasis.

Biochemical notes: Peptide, steroid, and amine hormones; receptor-mediated signaling (second messengers or gene transcription).

References: OpenStax Anatomy & Physiology.

Urinary System

Overview: Filters blood, removes waste, and maintains fluid/electrolyte/acid-base balance.

Anatomy: Kidneys, ureters, urinary bladder, urethra.

Main organs: Kidneys (nephrons are functional units).

Functions: Filtration, reabsorption, secretion; production of urine; renin and erythropoietin secretion.

References: OpenStax; renal physiology resources.

Immune & Lymphatic Systems

Overview: Defends against pathogens and returns interstitial fluid to circulation.

Anatomy: Lymph nodes, spleen, thymus, lymphatic vessels, bone marrow; innate and adaptive immune cells.

Functions: Pathogen recognition and elimination, immune memory, fluid balance.

Cellular notes: B and T lymphocytes, antibodies, cytokines.

References: OpenStax; NCBI immunology.

Integumentary System

Overview: Protective barrier, temperature regulation, sensory interface.

Anatomy: Skin (epidermis, dermis, hypodermis), hair, nails, glands.

Functions: Protection, thermoregulation, sensation, vitamin D synthesis initiation, minor excretion.

References: OpenStax Anatomy & Physiology.

Reproductive System

Overview: Produces gametes and sex hormones; supports offspring development (female).

Anatomy: Male (testes, ducts, accessory glands, penis); Female (ovaries, uterine tubes, uterus, vagina).

Functions: Gametogenesis, hormone production, fertilization support, pregnancy.

Note: Educational overview only. Individual medical questions require professional consultation.

References: OpenStax Anatomy & Physiology.
