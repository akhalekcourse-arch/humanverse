Biochemistry

Molecular foundations of life: DNA, proteins, metabolism, enzymes, and cellular energy.

Molecular Biology — DNA, RNA, Proteins

DNA stores genetic information as a double helix of nucleotides (A-T, G-C). Transcription produces mRNA; translation at ribosomes synthesizes proteins from mRNA codons. Proteins fold into functional 3D structures determined by amino acid sequence.

Key processes: Replication, transcription, translation, post-translational modification.

Metabolism — Glycolysis & Krebs Cycle

Glycolysis (cytosol): glucose → 2 pyruvate + net 2 ATP + 2 NADH. Under aerobic conditions, pyruvate enters mitochondria, is converted to acetyl-CoA, and enters the citric acid (Krebs) cycle, producing NADH, FADH₂, and CO₂.

Complete aerobic oxidation of one glucose molecule yields approximately 30–32 ATP.

Enzymology

Enzymes are biological catalysts (mostly proteins) that lower activation energy. They are specific, saturable, and regulated by inhibitors, allosteric effectors, pH, and temperature. Examples: hexokinase, pyruvate dehydrogenase, ATP synthase.

Human Chemistry — pH, Ions, Electrolytes

Blood pH is tightly regulated (~7.35–7.45). Major ions: Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺, Cl⁻, HCO₃⁻. Electrolyte balance is maintained by kidneys, hormones (aldosterone, ADH, PTH), and respiratory compensation.

Nutrition — Vitamins & Minerals

Vitamins act as coenzymes or antioxidants (e.g., B vitamins, vitamin C, vitamin D). Minerals (Ca, Fe, I, Zn, Mg) serve structural, catalytic, or signaling roles. Deficiencies produce characteristic clinical syndromes.

Cell Biology — Organelles & Membranes

Plasma membrane: phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins. Mitochondria: ATP production. Endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi: protein/lipid processing and trafficking. Lysosomes: degradation. Nucleus: genetic control.

ATP & Cellular Energy

ATP is the primary energy currency. Produced mainly by oxidative phosphorylation in mitochondria: electron transport chain creates proton gradient; ATP synthase uses proton flow to phosphorylate ADP. Oxygen is the final electron acceptor.

Formula note: ATP ⇌ ADP + Pᵢ + energy (hydrolysis releases ~30.5 kJ/mol under standard conditions; physiological free energy is higher).
